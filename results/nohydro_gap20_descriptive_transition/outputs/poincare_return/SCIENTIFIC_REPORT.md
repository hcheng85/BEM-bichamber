# Recurrent chamber motion and the Poincare-return hypothesis

## Decision

The repeated approach clock is generated by a real chamber-scale collective motion, but not by a stable periodic orbit with a closed two-dimensional Poincare map. The source school executes a coherent, wall-guided, strongly deformed sweep of the chamber. A typical no-entry approach recurs after approximately one half-winding, whereas a clean same-chirality return to the section takes approximately twice as long. The present section state contains reproducible return-state information, but it does not resolve return timing or transfer reward, and significant dependence on the preceding return remains. The proposed low-dimensional return map therefore fails its closure gate.

This is a useful physical separation: chamber geometry and coherent translation generate the encounter clock, while slowly evolving collective organization and interaction history determine whether an encounter transfers population. The data do not justify interpreting the motion as a deterministic limit cycle or using this map to predict the complete nine-event dominant-chamber relocations.

## Data, geometry, and fixed section

The analysis uses the existing 40 no-hydrodynamics, gap-20 trajectories and the established approach episodes. Seeds 0--29 are used for construction and seeds 30--39 only for held-out tests. No microscopic simulation was run.

The production boundary is read from the archived source (SHA-256 `5e57b9dca2cb962e2c35f540f3982d5f4018eb719067223e6b319866b4482284`) and split at the divider. Its two area centroids are at x = +/-3.25255; each chamber half has area 74.7471. The section is fixed before looking at outcomes: the outward horizontal ray from the exact source-chamber centroid, opposite the gap. Crossings are oriented by their observed chirality. The only section state is

\[
z=(r,\alpha),
\]

where `r` is source-school COM radius and `alpha` is mean-heading angle relative to the oriented tangent. Population imbalance is retained only as transfer bookkeeping and is not an input to the map. Terminal records and opposite-chirality crossings are not treated as ordinary same-direction returns.

The orbit cache contains 96,424 chamber samples at 0.5-time-unit spacing. The episode audit exactly recovers the stipulated 1,195 consecutive no-entry, zero-crossing pairs.

## What physical motion produces the clock?

There are 1,076 oriented section events. Among intervals with no sampled change in source population count, 191 are same-chirality returns. Their median periods are

| split | median return time | seed-bootstrap 95% interval | returns |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| train | 31.56 | [31.44, 31.65] | 168 |
| held out | 31.62 | [31.31, 32.05] | 23 |

By contrast, consecutive no-entry attempts recur after a median 15.5 in both training and held-out data. For the 147 held-out pairs retaining the same circulation direction, the median unwrapped phase advance is 0.464 winding; 91.8% lie between 0.35 and 0.65 winding and none between 0.8 and 1.2. Thus a complete winding does **not** produce one approach cycle. The observed clock is a half-orbit encounter scale, with about two approach opportunities per full same-chirality return.

The motion is recurrent but not a narrow circular orbit. Consecutive section crossings reverse chirality 57.7% of the time. In held-out constant-count returns, the median radial standard deviation is 2.03, the median tangent alignment is only 0.229, and the median directional phase fraction is 0.803. Trajectories form elongated, wall-guided loops with substantial radial deformation and reversals rather than small fluctuations around a deterministic periodic orbit. Because the cache records chamber counts rather than fish-identity masks, equal simultaneous exchanges inside a 0.5 sampling interval cannot be excluded; these returns must not be called exact fixed-membership intervals.

Short attempts after transfer have a separate geometric explanation. In held-out seeds, attempts following returned or partial-transfer episodes recur after medians 10.0 and 8.0, respectively, versus 15.0 after no entry and 16.0 after sustained transfer. These short recurrences occur while the school and approach layers remain near the gap, often with a direction change; they are additional encounters within the ongoing sweep, not evidence for a faster complete orbit. This also separates the approximately 9--11 recovery time from the 15.5 approach clock.

## Return-map validation

An affine map was used only as the minimal local diagnostic; no nonlinear map or potential was fitted. For all same-chirality pairs, including transfer intervals with population retained solely as bookkeeping, there are 305 training and 120 held-out returns. The current section state improves held-out, equal-seed normalized return-state MSE from 1.540 to 1.075, an improvement of 0.465 with seed-bootstrap 95% interval [0.298, 0.621]. Its pooled improvement, 0.491, exceeds every one of 500 shuffled-return controls (shuffle 95% range [-0.183, 0.130]). The section therefore contains genuine state-to-state structure.

That structure is insufficient for a closed map:

- Return time is not improved reliably over a constant-period baseline: MSE improvement 28.5, 95% interval [-53.3, 112.1].
- Adding the immediately preceding section state improves held-out return-state MSE by 0.140, 95% interval [0.058, 0.248], across all ten held-out seeds. Earlier-return memory therefore remains after conditioning on `(r,alpha)`.
- Although an empirical affine fixed point and eigenvalues can be calculated, they have no warranted dynamical interpretation once the closure test fails. Fixed-point stability is consequently not claimed.

The section also fails to predict the next physical outcome. In held-out seeds, improvement over constant baselines is unresolved for encounter occurrence (0.00253, 95% interval [-0.00242, 0.00784]), signed population transfer (-0.0197, [-0.336, 0.321]), and sustained-transfer classification (-0.000145, [-0.00228, 0.00182]). These are prospective tests attached to the subsequent encounter, not retrospective descriptions.

## Physical interpretation and implications

The Poincare hypothesis succeeds only at the level of encounter scheduling. The production geometry and coherent aggregate translation support a chamber-scale sweep whose half-winding sets the approximately 15.5 approach recurrence. This gives a physical origin for the encounter-frequency scale without assuming Poisson attempts or fitting an attempt-time distribution. It does not, however, predict individual return times more accurately than the constant physical-period baseline.

The map fails precisely where a transition theory needs it: its two variables are not Markovian, and they carry no independently resolved information about transfer reward. This is consistent with the preceding causal and time-scale analyses: directed visual interactions influence collective turning and sustained supply, while relative-heading organization does not relax within one approach. That unresolved organizational history can persist across the chosen section even when COM position and mean heading recur.

Exact population and approach-region conservation still determine how a realized encounter's flux changes storage and chamber population. They do not determine the distribution of encounter rewards. Complete opposite-chamber relocation is a sequence of such encounters and remains too sparsely observed to validate a separate rate from this map.

The supported minimal description is therefore a recurrent geometric encounter clock coupled to a history-dependent commitment process, not a closed deterministic return map. A renewal or semi-Markov representation may summarize that history empirically, but the present evidence does not yet derive its commitment kernel from first principles. Per the prespecified stopping rule, no extra section coordinates or alternative sections were introduced to force closure.
